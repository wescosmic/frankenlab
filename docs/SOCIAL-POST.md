## Main version

```
#KiroweenHackathon26

FrankenLab: a scientific sandbox where electronics, biology, materials and
chemistry share one simulation.

A plant drinks a soil dry. The soil's moisture changes a probe's resistance.
The probe sits on the tap of a voltage divider. When the soil runs dry the
comparator trips and the LED lights.

No discipline knows about any other. They share one component graph, one
clock and one kernel that has never heard of any of them.

Three experiments ship:
- the flagship, where the soil decides the day the alarm goes off
- the same circuit with the biology taken out
- a catalyst that gets a reaction to equilibrium nine times sooner, and
  provably does not move the equilibrium at all

Every answer the assistant gives ends with what it is worth: the weakest
model in the experiment, named, with a plain-English explanation of what its
confidence level means. A number the simulation did not produce is removed
from the reply, and the removal is stated. The one exemption is a whole
number at or below 1, because "day 1" is writing, not a measurement.

Built with Kiro. The part worth watching: a Kiro science-reviewer agent
checked our soil physics against published data, found a field capacity that
was below the published range, and the whole timeline moved three days. The
audit was the product.

No API key. No network calls. Runs in a browser.

Demo: <paste the YouTube link here>
Code: https://github.com/wescosmic/frankenlab
```

## Short version

```
#KiroweenHackathon26

FrankenLab: four sciences sharing one simulation.

A plant dries a soil. The soil drives a resistive probe. The probe sits on a
voltage divider, so the comparator thresholds a solved circuit quantity, not
the moisture that drove it. Change the reference resistor and the day the
alarm fires moves with it.

Three experiments, every number measured by a script rather than written by
hand, and an assistant that ends every answer by naming the weakest model in
the experiment.

Built with Kiro, including a science-reviewer agent that caught our soil
physics being wrong against published data and moved the whole timeline.

Demo: <paste the YouTube link here>
Code: https://github.com/wescosmic/frankenlab
```
